## Paper summary
Multi-agent LLM systems face a familiar engineering problem: building reliable systems from individually unreliable parts. The paper develops an operational mapping from Erlang/OTP constructs to agent systems and tests supervision as a capability multiplier. It grades twenty mappings as exact, loose, or breaking; these grades describe the correspondence, rather than guarantees implemented by an agent runtime.
The study examines supervision across model tiers, matched-compute controls, and clean-context decomposition. Its limits include semantic failure detection, context leakage, and restart economics. The published paper is the source of truth for the claims; results depend on the evaluated tasks, models, and controls, and the matched-compute verdicts are directional: neither run certified compute parity. The matched fixed-token and fixed-cost comparisons in the separately provisioned scaling trial were withheld by their preregistered validity gate; the reproduced accuracy curve does not establish matched-cost savings.
